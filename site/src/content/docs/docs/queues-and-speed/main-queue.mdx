title: The main queue
description: Ladle runs a few downloads at the same time and lines up the rest, so each file gets a fair share of your connection.

## How it works

Everything you start goes into the main queue. Ladle runs **3 downloads at the same time** out of the box; the rest wait in line and start one by one as slots free up. Waiting downloads show **Next in line** with their size.

To see only what’s waiting, click **Main queue** under **Queues** in Ladle’s sidebar.

## Change how many run at once

Open **Ladle › Settings › Speed** and set **Downloads at the same time**, anywhere from 1 to 8.

- **Fewer** gives each file more of your bandwidth, so the first ones finish sooner.
- **More** helps when you’re fetching lots of small files, or the servers are slow on their own.

Each download also splits into several connections. See [Connections and speed limits](/docs/queues-and-speed/connections-and-speed-limits/).

## Start, pause and resume

| Do this | How |
| --- | --- |
| Start a waiting download right away | Select it, then click **Start now** |
| Pause or resume the selected download | <kbd>Space</kbd> |
| Pause or resume everything | <kbd>⌥⌘P</kbd> |
| Retry a failed download | <kbd>⌘R</kbd> |

A paused download keeps its place. When you resume it, every connection picks up where it stopped.

## On battery and Low Data networks

Ladle can step back when your Mac needs to save power or data. Both switches are in **Ladle › Settings › Schedule** and are on out of the box:

- **Pause when running on battery** pauses Ladle’s queues while your Mac runs on battery, and carries on when you plug it back in.
- **Pause on Low Data networks** pauses them on networks where macOS Low Data Mode is on, like a phone’s hotspot.

To turn on Low Data Mode for a network, open **System Settings › Wi-Fi**, click **Details…** next to the network, and turn on **Low Data Mode**.
